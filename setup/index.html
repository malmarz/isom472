Setup guide

Install the software and create the accounts before the setup session.

ISOM 472 › Setup guide

The software and the accounts on this page must be ready before the setup session in our next class, 08:00, in Room 1001. Exact dates are announced in class and on the course site. The session confirms that the software runs on your own laptop, so it has to be installed when you arrive.

Do this at home, on home wifi. The download is large and the classroom wifi is shared by the whole class. It will not complete on the morning of the session.

There are three parts: software to install, accounts to create, and the roles the team divides between its members. Work through them in order. The first two are done at home, before the session. The third is settled with your team and sent to the instructor during the session itself. Each step says what you should see when it has worked.

Part 1 — Software to install

Antigravity

Go to https://antigravity.google/download and download the entry named Antigravity 2.0 — version 2.13.0 at the time of writing.

Further down the same page there is a separate, older entry called "Antigravity IDE". That is not the one used in this course. Check the name before you download.

Installing on Windows

  1. On the download page, choose the x64 Windows download. Choose ARM64 only if you know the laptop has an ARM processor.
  2. Open the downloaded .exe file from the Downloads folder.
  3. If a blue box appears saying "Windows protected your PC", choose More info, then Run anyway. This appears because the application is new, not because anything is wrong.
  4. Follow the installer and let it finish.

You should see Antigravity listed in the Start menu, and it should open in its own window.

Installing on macOS

  1. On the download page, choose Apple Silicon unless you know the Mac has an Intel processor. To check: Apple menu, then About This Mac, which names the chip.
  2. Open the downloaded .dmg file.
  3. Drag the Antigravity icon onto the Applications folder shown in the same window.
  4. Open the Applications folder and double-click Antigravity.
  5. If macOS says the developer cannot be verified, open System Settings, then Privacy & Security, scroll down, and choose Open Anyway.

You should see Antigravity open in its own window.

Macs with an Intel processor

Google's documentation states that Antigravity supports Apple Silicon only, while its download page offers an Intel build and lists "Apple Silicon or Intel". These two Google pages contradict each other, and the contradiction is unresolved.

If your Mac is Intel, download the Intel build and try it. If it does not install or does not open, stop and bring the laptop to the setup session.

GitHub Desktop

Install GitHub Desktop as well, from https://desktop.github.com/. It is how you will move work between your laptop and the team repository without using a terminal.

You should see GitHub Desktop open and ask you to sign in to GitHub. Signing in can wait until you have created the account in Part 2.

What the laptop needs

Minimum operating system, as published by Google.
Item Requirement

Part 2 — Accounts

Three accounts are involved, and they do not all work the same way. Read the difference carefully: one is issued to you, one is individual, and one is shared by the whole team.

Who holds each account.
Account How many Used for
KU account The one you already have Signs in to both Gemini Enterprise and Antigravity
GitHub One per student The team repository, and the record of your own work
Supabase One per team, shared The team's database and its sign-ins

KU account

Your Kuwait University account signs you in to both Gemini Enterprise and Antigravity. It is the only Google account needed for this course. Do not create a personal Gmail account for it.

  1. Open Antigravity.
  2. Choose Continue with Google.
  3. Sign in with your KU account and finish the short setup.

You should see the main Antigravity window with its chat panel. Close it. On the evening before the session, open it once more and confirm it is still signed in.

GitHub — one account each

Every student needs an individual GitHub account. Sign up at https://github.com/signup.

When the account exists, write down your GitHub username — the username, not the email address. Your username is what identifies your work in the team repository for the whole semester, so bring it with you.

You should see your username at the top of github.com when signed in, and the same account signed in inside GitHub Desktop.

Supabase — one account per team

Supabase is the database. Each team creates a single Supabase account and shares it. This is not one account per student. Decide within the team who creates it, then make sure every member can sign in to it.

Sign up at https://supabase.com/dashboard/sign-up. It is free, it works in Kuwait, and it does not ask for a card.

The team repository

The instructor creates the repository for each team. Teams do not create their own, and there is nothing to set up on GitHub beyond your individual account. The order is:

  1. Every student creates a GitHub account and notes the username, as in the step above.
  2. The team fills in the team roster workbook: one row per member, giving each member's role and the team's Phase Lead rotation. The roles are described in Part 3 below.
  3. The team's Phase 1 Lead emails the completed workbook to the instructor at mo.almarzouq@ku.edu.kw. One email per team, not one per student.
  4. The instructor creates the team repository and gives the team access.
  5. All work for the rest of the semester happens in that repository.

Download the workbook here: isom472-team-roster.xlsx. It opens in Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets.

The workbook is due before you leave the setup session. Filled in and emailed, not finished later at home. Until it arrives the team has no repository, so nothing else in the course can start. Exact dates are announced in class and on the course site.

Nothing in this course requires a purchase, a paid plan or a credit card. If a screen asks for payment, stop and ask before continuing — you are on the wrong page or the wrong plan.

Part 3 — Team roles

Every team divides the same work between its members. Each role owns one part of the system and produces one artifact, so at any point in the semester it is clear who is answerable for what. Settle the roles with your team at the setup session and record them in the roster workbook.

Standing roles

A team of four takes the first four roles. A fifth member takes a combined Quality and FinOps Lead. At six, that combined role splits into a FinOps Lead and a Quality Lead.

The standing roles, what each one owns, and the artifact it produces.
Role Owns Artifact
Client Lead The client relationship and what the system must do The backlog: user stories with acceptance criteria
Design Lead What it looks like and how it flows The HTML prototype and screen list
Data Lead The data and its structure The schema and seed data in Supabase
Build Lead That it actually runs The deployed system at its public address, plus release notes
FinOps Lead What the AI work costs, and what changed as a result The Sprint FinOps Ledger
Quality Lead Finding defects in the running application Bug issues with steps to reproduce, tracked to a disposition

The Phase Lead — the rotating role

Alongside the standing role each member keeps all semester, one named person leads each phase. There are six phases, so a team of six has everyone lead once, a team of five means one member leads twice, and a team of four means two members do.

The Phase Lead runs the phase: keeps the board current, makes sure the delivery lands on time, and is the instructor's point of contact for that phase. The artifact is the phase delivery note. In a team of four, the Phase Lead also keeps that phase's ledger and files that phase's bug tickets, so both responsibilities rotate with the lead.

Decide the whole rotation at the setup session, not one phase at a time. The roster workbook asks for all six.

What holds for everyone

Some rules apply regardless of role. Two of them matter from the first day; the full list is on the team roles page.

  • Every member delivers at least two named stories end to end each sprint. A role is what you own, not the only thing you do.
  • Every member reviews teammates' pull requests. Nobody approves their own.

What to bring

  • Your laptop, charged, and its charger. Room 1001 does not have enough outlets for everyone.
  • Antigravity and GitHub Desktop already installed.
  • Sign-in details for all three accounts, reachable from the laptop you bring — not saved only on a phone, and not a password that would have to be reset during the session.
  • Your GitHub username, written down.
  • A few gigabytes of free disk space.

Troubleshooting

Common problems and what to do about them.
What you see What to do
Windows: a blue "Windows protected your PC" box Choose More info, then Run anyway. Expected for a new application.
macOS: the developer cannot be verified Open System Settings, then Privacy & Security, scroll down, and choose Open Anyway.
The download stalls or fails partway Start it again on home wifi rather than on phone data or campus wifi. Do not retry it during the session.
It installs but will not open, or closes immediately Restart the laptop and try once more. If it fails again, stop and bring the laptop to the session.
The Mac has an Intel processor Google's own pages disagree about Intel support. Try the Intel build; if it fails, bring the laptop to the session.

If it will not install

In this order:

  1. Bring it to the setup session. Fixing installations that failed is a planned part of the session, not an interruption of it.
  2. Pair with a teammate for that session and get your own machine working afterwards.
  3. Take the browser-based route. Every part of this course can be done in a browser, with no installed software at all. It is a full route through the course, not a reduced one.

The one thing to avoid is arriving at the session having tried nothing.
