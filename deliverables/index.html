What each delivery contains

Phase 1 — team, environment, and an accepted proposal

  • A formed team, with roles recorded on the roster workbook.
  • A verified environment — the tools installed and signed in.
  • A proposal the instructor accepts, in the required shape — as docs/proposal.md, and published as a page from the team's repository.
  • Rejected proposals come back within three calendar days.
  • The first resubmission carries no penalty; a second caps that component at half.

No team proceeds past Wed 30 Sep 2026 (the end of week 3) without an accepted proposal. At that point the instructor assigns a project from a standing list, the proposal component scores zero, and the rest of Phase 1 is unaffected — the team is not sunk.

Phase 2 — design sprint: stories, the schema, work split, token plan

  • A backlog at or above the floor, ordered, with acceptance criteria.
  • Acceptance criteria that say what one member's work hands to another's, using the column names in the schema.
  • The split — who holds which named stories this sprint.
  • The schema, with keys marked and row-level security enabled on every table.
  • A verified environment, working for every member.
  • The token plan, and the first ledger entry.
  • Presented to the room, with questions from the room.

The backlog floor. Two stories per member per sprint, across four sprints.

The backlog floor by team size.
Team size Backlog floor
Four 32 stories
Five 40 stories
Six 48 stories

It is a floor, not a scope target. Below it the individual mark has nothing to attach to.

The token plan. It says five things.

  • Where each kind of work happens — planned in the browser, built on the machine.
  • Which model for which task, and why that one.
  • The tactics the team will use to keep a run narrow.
  • What you will watch, and what you have no way of seeing.
  • What you do when the quota runs out, decided before it does.

It lives in the first section of docs/finops-ledger.md, committed like everything else. Short and specific beats long and general.

Phase 3 — sprint 1: the first working increment

The most guided sprint.

  • A working increment off the backlog.
  • Reviewed merges — no one merges their own work unread.
  • The board matching the git log.
  • A current ledger.
  • The phase tag in the repository.

Phase 4 — sprint 2: the second working increment

As Phase 3. Nothing new is introduced.

Phase 5 — sprint 3: the third increment

As Phase 3, plus the analytics feature specified in week 9, run on the team's own data.

Phase 6 — final sprint and the demo

Two parts, two dates.

  • The final sprint, due Wed 9 Dec 2026: the frozen scope shipped, analytics included, delivered exactly like phases 3 to 5.
  • The demo, Mon 14 and Wed 16 Dec 2026: the system shown working, with oral questions to individuals. It is scored by the instructor and by peer assessment, and the detailed rubric is issued before the demo.

The repository tag and the FinOps ledger

Two rules. They start at different phases.

  • Every phase is tagged in the repository at its due date, and the tag is what gets graded. All six, phase-1 through phase-6, from Phase 1 onward. Not a submitted document, not a slide deck — the repository at that tag. See the repository page for the tag names and the rest of the repository rules.
  • The FinOps ledger is part of every delivery from Phase 2 onward. It is graded binary: present, current, and plausible against the git log, or zero. There is no accuracy target and no arithmetic to do.

How a delivery session works

A delivery session is not a queue of private meetings. All teams are in the room. Each team presents, and the rest of the class is the audience and the question pool. Teams arrive with their own questions prepared — questions for the teams presenting, about what they built and the choices behind it. Arriving without them is itself a failure of the session. Every delivery is presented from a public address, not from a laptop.

Templates a delivery uses

  • The project proposal — the required shape.
  • Your repository — the rules and the layout.
  • Publishing from your repository.
  • Team roles.
  • The team roster workbook (.xlsx).
