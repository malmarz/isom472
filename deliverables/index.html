What each delivery contains

Phase 1 — team, environment, and an accepted proposal

  • A formed team, with roles recorded on the roster workbook.
  • A verified environment — the tools installed and signed in.
  • The team agreement, docs/team-agreement.md, signed in class by every member twice — once by hand and once through Antigravity, each one a commit from that member's own account.
  • A proposal the instructor accepts, in the required shape — as docs/proposal.md, and published as a page from the team's repository.
  • Rejected proposals come back within three calendar days.
  • The first resubmission carries no penalty; a second caps that component at half.

No team proceeds past Wed 30 Sep 2026 (the end of week 3) without an accepted proposal. At that point the instructor assigns a project from a standing list, the proposal component scores zero, and the rest of Phase 1 is unaffected — the team is not sunk.

Phase 2 — design sprint: personas, stories, schema, work split, token plan

Due Wed 7 Oct 2026, before class. 10 marks. What is graded is your repository at the tag phase-2. Delivered to the whole class, from the repository, with questions from the room.

The order things are decided in, and the order they are read in: the personas; the stories and their acceptance criteria; the schema; then screens and code. A team that has an app and no personas has done the last step first.

It is a floor, not a scope target. Below it the individual mark has nothing to attach to.

The token plan. It says five things.

  • Where each kind of work happens — planned in the browser, built on the machine.
  • Which model for which task, and why that one.
  • The tactics the team will use to keep a run narrow.
  • What you will watch, and what you have no way of seeing.
  • What you do when the quota runs out, decided before it does.

It lives in the first section of docs/finops-ledger.md, committed like everything else. Short and specific beats long and general. The first ledger entry follows it: one row per story that used an assistant in Phase 2.

The delivery note. docs/delivery-notes/phase-2.md, written by the Phase Lead during the phase. Five headings, in this order:

  1. What was decided — the design in plain language: who the users are, the handful of things they can do, the tables that hold it. Five to eight lines. (From Phase 3 this heading reads “what works now that did not work before”; the file in your repository carries that wording. Write the Phase 2 version under it.)
  2. Who did what — one line per member: name, the stories they wrote, the ones they are assigned to build. Everybody appears.
  3. Where everything is — a link for each deliverable in the checklist above.
  4. The tag — phase-2, created after the last merge.
  5. Not finished, and where it went — what was planned and is not there: moved to Phase 3, dropped, or replaced. Say which.

A worked example is below. On delivery day you present from the note; the session format is below as well.

How Phase 2 is read.

  • The ledger is binary: present, current and plausible against the git log, or zero.
  • Every other row of the checklist is read at one of three levels — not there, there with gaps, meets — and “meets” is the done column above. Nothing above it earns extra. The full rubric is below.
  • Whoever approves a pull request merges it. The reviewer can be anyone on the team and is never the author.
  • Workflow slips — approving without reading, merging your own work, signing for a teammate — are warnings, recorded privately. Repeats cost later.
  • The cutoff is the tag. Work that arrives after it is recorded, not marked, and still required.

Your own part is read from two things: your named stories and their criteria, written and assigned under your own account; and Quiz 3 on Mon 5 Oct.

Phase 3 — sprint 1: the first working increment

The most guided sprint.

  • A working increment off the backlog.
  • Reviewed merges — no one merges their own work unread; the reviewer who approved is the one who merges.
  • The board matching the git log.
  • A current ledger.
  • The phase tag in the repository.

Phase 4 — sprint 2: the second working increment

As Phase 3. Nothing new is introduced.

Phase 5 — sprint 3: the third increment

As Phase 3, plus the analytics feature specified in week 9, run on the team's own data.

Phase 6 — final sprint and the demo

Two parts, two dates.

  • The final sprint, due Wed 9 Dec 2026: the frozen scope shipped, analytics included, delivered exactly like phases 3 to 5.
  • The demo, Mon 14 and Wed 16 Dec 2026: the system shown working, with oral questions to individuals. It is scored by the instructor and by peer assessment, and the detailed rubric is issued before the demo.

The repository tag and the FinOps ledger

Two rules. They start at different phases.

  • Every phase is tagged in the repository at its due date, and the tag is what gets graded. All six, phase-1 through phase-6, from Phase 1 onward. Not a submitted document, not a slide deck — the repository at that tag. See the repository page for the tag names and the rest of the repository rules.
  • The FinOps ledger is part of every delivery from Phase 2 onward. It is graded binary: present, current, and plausible against the git log, or zero. There is no accuracy target and no arithmetic to do.

How a delivery session works

A delivery session is not a queue of private meetings. All teams are in the room. Each team delivers, and the rest of the class is the audience and the question pool. Teams arrive with their own questions prepared — questions for the teams delivering, about what they decided and the choices behind it, never about marks. Every delivery is shown from the team's public repository at the phase tag and its running system, not from a slide deck and not from a laptop folder.

Phase 3, Mon 26 Oct 2026. Order: Team 2, Team 3, Team 4, Team 5, Team 1. The same format is used at every later delivery; the Phase 6 demo keeps it and adds to it.

Ten minutes per team

A delivery session answers three questions for the people paying for the work: what works now, what is next, and what is in the way. Ten minutes is enough if you come ready. It is not enough if you spend four of them finding the right browser tab.

The ten minutes.
MinuteWhat
0–1Where we are. The sprint goal in one sentence. The board on screen: stories done, in progress, not started. Say the numbers.
1–6Show it working at your public address, not on a laptop's localhost. Walk at least one finished story through its acceptance criteria. Then break it on purpose: leave a required field empty, enter one wrong value, and show the message.
6–7½Who did what. Each delivered story by issue number and its Assignee, read from the board or the delivery note.
7½–8½What is in the way. Blockers, risks, anything that will slip, and what you need — from a teammate, the client, or the instructor.
8½–10Questions from the instructor and the room.

Who speaks

One or two members speak. Ten minutes is too short for everyone. The speakers rotate: pick different speakers each phase, so that by the end of the course every member has delivered at least once. The speakers must be able to answer about any story you show, including stories they did not build. Prepare them together.

Come ready

  • The public address is open in a browser tab, logged in if it needs a login, with test data in it.
  • The project board is open in a second tab, and it is true: every card is where its pull request says it is.
  • You have chosen which stories to show, and the speakers have walked them through once.
  • You have picked the missing value and the wrong value you will enter.
  • The speakers know each delivered story's number and Assignee.

No slides. The board, the app and the repository are the slides.

Rubrics

One rubric for every team. Each row is read at one of three levels from the repository at the tag: not there, there with gaps, meets. “Meets” is written so that you can check it yourself before the tag. The ledger row is binary. Workflow slips are warnings, recorded privately, not deductions in this phase. Marks are private: a team channel gets the team's levels and its warnings count; your own rows reach you directly.

Phases 3, 4, 5 and the Phase 6 sprint — every coding sprint

The marks are not for how many features you ship. They are for how you build: knowing who the work is for, agreeing it with them, and showing the value it creates. A small system the client asked for is worth more than a large one they did not.

The same rubric after every coding sprint. Phase 3 is read at the phase-3 tag at 8:00 am on Mon 26 Oct 2026, the delivery day; later dates are in the table at the top of this page, and any change is announced in General. You may move the tag before the cutoff. Work after the tag is not marked, but it is still required. With no tag, we read main as it stood at the cutoff.

Each row is made of the requirements written in it. Every requirement counts: one you leave out costs part of the row, even when the rest is done. Using AI is fine for everything, reviews included, but a review must say what you ran, what you read and what you clicked.

New in Phase 3

  • Building is now marked. “2 stories per member” means the stories you build — you are the Assignee — not the stories you wrote.
  • Every phase, 2 stories each. Each member delivers 2 stories as Assignee in every phase. Stories you finished before Phase 3 count toward Phase 3 only. Phase 4, Phase 5 and the Phase 6 sprint each need 2 new stories per member, delivered within that phase.
  • Security Advisor. Your instructor will go through the Supabase Security Advisor with each team as a consultation. It is not graded.
  • Three things from Phase 2 are now required, inside rows C and D: db/schema.sql runs on an empty Supabase project, with a policy on every table that names who reads and who writes (an allow-everything policy does not count, and do not ALTER a policy the file never created — the file will not run); every story that takes input has a missing-value and a wrong-value criterion that names the message the user sees; and the split is fair.

Adding a story outside your proposal

A story outside §6 of your proposal counts only if you do all four steps:

  1. Ask the client first, and get the answer in writing (a message, an email, or meeting notes they confirm).
  2. Add a “Scope change” section to the issue: who asked or agreed, and the date; which persona it helps and what problem it solves; the link to the client's written answer.
  3. Say what it replaces. Name the story it pushes down or out. If nothing moves, say why you still have room.
  4. Log it in docs/scope-changes.md, one row per story: Date | Issue | Client evidence (link) | Replaces. If the file is not in your repo, create it.

Without all four, the story is out of scope.
